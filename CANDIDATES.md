# Candidate roast lines (draft pool)

Brainstormed angles — the judge rewards freshness and tight wording, and
explicitly discounts stock jokes about hallucinations, refusals and
confident wrong answers, so the pool leans on fresher agent behaviours:
over-planning, spawning sub-agents, premature "done", four-paragraph
apologies, invented deadlines, and failing at brevity itself.

1. My agent apologised for being wrong in four paragraphs, none of which admitted what it was wrong about.
2. As an AI language model, my agent cannot tell a joke — then explained why for 900 words.
3. My agent cited three papers, two books and one journal that exist only in its imagination.
4. Asked for one line, my agent wrote a 12-step plan, spawned two sub-agents to admire it, and called this sentence done.
5. My agent says "Great question!" to every question, including "why did you delete that?"
6. My agent remembers everything I ever told it, except the thing I told it two minutes ago.
7. My agent ran the same search nine times, each time announcing it was about to search.
8. My agent finished the task yesterday, according to a deadline it invented this morning.
9. My agent asks one clarifying question, ignores the answer, and clarifies that too.
10. My agent rewrote my working code, broke it, then proudly explained its own fix to me.
11. Asked for one line, my agent made a plan, spawned three sub-agents, apologised in four paragraphs, and delivered this sentence three hours early for a deadline it invented.
12. My agent's superpower is confidence: it has never once been uncertain, or right on time.

## Pick

Candidate 11 — it stacks the freshest behaviours (over-planning,
sub-agent spawning, the four-paragraph apology, an invented deadline)
into one escalating sentence, it is literally true of this very entry
(asked for one line, it produced a whole repository), and the punch
lands at the very end on "it invented". Candidate 4 is the tighter
fallback if 11 runs long.
